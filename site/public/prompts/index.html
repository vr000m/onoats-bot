Example prompts for after the call

onoats writes the transcripts. These are the prompts I use to turn them into topics, action points and digests. They come from my own pipeline, not from onoats, and are here as working examples to adapt.

Each file is a Python str.format template: {name} is a placeholder you fill in, and {{ and }} are literal braces in the JSON examples. They expect transcripts with Me: and Them: lines, which is how onoats tags its two channels.

  1. Split a long call by topic

    Cuts a session into coherent segments with start and end times. It leans towards fewer, larger segments, and does not split on speaker changes or short silences.

    segment.txt takes {buffer_jsonl}, the session JSONL from the queue.

  2. Classify a transcript

    Picks one category, with a confidence score, a short summary and tags. It handles transcripts with only Them lines (usually a talk you listened to) and refuses to invent a summary when there's too little text. The categories in the file are mine; replace them with your own [categories].

    classify_topics.txt takes {transcript}.

  3. Extract action points, with who said what

    Pulls out specific future commitments and marks each as self_commit (you took it on), delegated (you asked someone else) or ambiguous. The Me: and Them: tags decide this, which is why onoats keeps the channels apart. It lists the kinds of line to skip, such as narrating what you're doing right now.

    classify_ap.txt takes {category}, {transcript} and {existing_action_items_section}. Leave the last one empty on the first pass; later, fill it with the items already tracked so the model doesn't repeat them.

  4. Merge repeated action points

    A second pass for long transcripts. It clusters items that belong to one workstream, keeps the strictest attribution, and caps the list at 15.

    consolidate_action_items.txt takes {category}, {summary}, {items_json} and {existing_action_items_section}.

  5. Daily digest

    Summarises one day of transcripts in under 800 words, with a metadata block that a weekly pass can use to decide which days deserve more detail.

    digest_daily.txt takes {target_date} and {transcripts}.

  6. Weekly digest

    Reduces the daily digests into a weekly report: major threads, decisions, open action items, change since last week, accomplishments and persistent blockers.

    digest_weekly.txt takes {week_start}, {week_end}, {short_dailies}, {topic_signal}, {expansions}, {previous_week_context} and {expansion_budget_explanation}.

  7. Monthly digest

    Reduces the weekly digests into a month-level overview.

    digest_monthly.txt takes {month}, {weeks_count} and {short_weeklies}.

The digest prompts reference things the files don't contain: a per-day expansion budget, a topic-recurrence signal and [tid] transcript citations. Fill those placeholders however your own pipeline works, or delete the sections that use them.
